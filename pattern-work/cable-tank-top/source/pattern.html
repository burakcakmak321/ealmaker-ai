04 MOTIF CHARTS

16-row master repeat. Odd (RS) row numbers are at the right edge; read these rows from right to left, starting at column 1. Even (WS) row numbers are at the left edge; read these rows from left to right. Numbers under each chart are column numbers within the motif; column 1 is the rightmost cell.

k p C4F C4B C6F T3F T3B repeat rows 1–16

The Diamond 10 motif is a travelling-cable diamond. Its crossings are arranged so T3B is only worked across P-K-K and T3F only across K-K-P (in working order). This keeps the two knit strands travelling cleanly through a six-stitch purl background without changing the stitch count.

Rope 6 and Honeycomb 8 are all-knit cable fields on the RS; their WS rows are purled. Diamond background stitches remain purl on the RS and knit on the WS.

Neckline ruleNever work a cable crossing unless all stitches required for that crossing are still present. From the neck split row on (including the split row), work any Diamond that has lost stitches in its knit/purl texture without crossings. Rope cables stay complete and are crossed normally.

07 HEM AND STRAIGHT BODY – FRONT AND BACK

Front and back are worked the same way except for the first rib row and the set-up of the seed fields, given separately below.

Rows 1–12: lower rib

With 4.0 mm (US 6) needles, CO 80 (92) sts.

  • Front, row 1 (RS): *k2, p2; rep from * to end.
  • Back, row 1 (RS): *p2, k2; rep from * to end.
  • Both pieces, rows 2–12: work the rib as established – knit the knit stitches and purl the purl stitches as they appear. Do not alternate the rib sequence by row.

The front and back start the rib differently so that, after one stitch from each piece is taken into each side seam, the k2, p2 rib runs continuously around the body. Checkpoint: 80 (92) sts.

Row 13 (RS): establish the body pattern

Change to 4.5 mm (US 7) needles. Work panel row 1 from the chart (section 05) or the written instructions (section 06). The seed fields are listed in working order: “first field” is worked at the start of the RS row, “second field” after the panel.

Seed-stitch side fields

  • The first and second fields are mirror images of each other, so the texture is mirrored at the panel edges. Front and back fields are set up opposite to each other so the seed stitch also continues unbroken across the side seams.
  • Row 14 (WS) and all following rows: p1 (selvedge), work seed st to marker by working the opposite of the stitch you see (purl a knit V, knit a purl bump), sm, work the next panel row, sm, seed st to last st, p1 (selvedge). On RS rows, the selvedge sts are k1. The selvedge sts are always worked in stockinette (knit on RS, purl on WS).
  • Keep the two panel markers 54 stitches apart. Armhole shaping removes only side-field stitches, so the cable panel remains intact until neckline shaping.

Work straight

Continue in pattern as set through global row 86 (88) – this is pattern row 10 (12), a WS row. 80 (92) sts remain.

Length check: the piece now measures approx. 35.8 (36.7) cm / 14.1 (14.4) in from the CO edge, measured flat. Work by the row numbers, not by length: the row numbers set the cable phase for the shaping. If your row gauge differs from 24 rows per 10 cm, the length to the underarm (and the total length) will differ in proportion.

CheckpointBefore beginning armholes, verify the total stitch count, 80 (92) sts; 54 stitches between the panel markers; 13 (19) sts outside each marker (including the selvedge st); and the cable-pattern row phase (last row worked = pattern row 10 (12)).

Armhole bind-offs

On each BO row, bind off 4 sts in pattern; the stitch on the right needle after the last BO counts as the first stitch of the rest of the row. From now on the new edge stitch at each armhole is a selvedge stitch: knit on RS, purl on WS.

Armhole decreases

On each listed RS decrease row: k1, ssk; maintain the remaining seed field; work the complete 54-st panel; maintain the opposite seed field to the last 3 sts; k2tog, k1. Each decrease row removes exactly 2 stitches. Work every WS row in between without decreases (p1, pattern to last st, p1). Checkpoint after the last decrease: 64 (76) sts – 54 panel sts and 5 (11) sts outside each marker.

Back neck split

On row 127 (133): work 18 (22) sts and slip them to a holder; BO the next 28 (32) sts in pattern without crossings (the st on the right needle after the last BO counts as the first of the remaining sts); work to end – 18 (22) sts. Checkpoint: 18 (22) / 28 (32) bound off / 18 (22).

Working the two back shoulders

After the centre neck bind-off, continue the second shoulder first (the sts on the needle). Work its next row as the required WS row (row 128 (134)) and continue to the final WS row 134 (140), then bind off all 18 (22) sts in pattern on RS row 135 (141). Rejoin yarn with WS facing at the neck edge of the held first shoulder and work the same row numbers so both shoulder tops finish at exactly the same height.

Partial motifsSize M only: 2 sts of each Diamond remain on each back shoulder. Work these remaining stitches in their established knit/purl texture, without crossings, until the shoulder bind-off. Size L: the back-neck BO removes both Diamonds completely; no partial motif remains.

10 FINISHING AND RIBBED BANDS

Blocking and seams

  • Wet-block front and back separately to the target measurements (section 01). Let both pieces dry completely before seaming.
  • Sew shoulder bound-off edges with a firm backstitch seam, right sides together. Match the front and back shoulder row ends exactly.
  • Sew side seams with mattress stitch, one full stitch in from the edge. Match global rows so the cable columns and lower rib remain level. With one stitch from each piece taken into each seam, the lower rib and the seed stitch run continuously across both side seams.

Horizontal bound-off edges are picked up approximately 1 stitch per bound-off stitch. Along row-end edges pick up slightly fewer than 3 sts for every 4 rows, as given in the table, so the band sits snugly. The totals are multiples of 4 for the 2×2 rib.

Ribbing

For both armholes and the neckline: work 6 rounds of *k2, p2; rep from * around. Bind off loosely in rib pattern. If a band visibly pulls inward or flares after a trial round, redistribute the pickups and adjust the total by a multiple of 4 rather than forcing the edge flat during blocking.

Final finishing

  • Weave in all ends on the WS, along the seams where possible.
  • Lightly steam or wet-block the bands so they lie flat; let the garment dry flat.

Cable audit checkpoints

  • Every centre panel row uses exactly 54 stitches. Cable crossings rearrange stitches but never increase or decrease the panel count.
  • Diamond 10 always contains four travelling knit stitches and six purl-background stitches. T3B must meet P-K-K; T3F must meet K-K-P (in working order). If that setup is not present, stop and correct the previous cable row.
  • Rope 6 and Honeycomb 8 contain only knit stitches on the RS. On WS rows these fields are purled.
  • Front and back shoulder counts and final row numbers must match before seaming: M = 18 sts and row 135; L = 22 sts and row 141.

Problem solving

  • Cable panel count is wrong: undo to the last incorrect crossing. Do not repair a cable-count problem by adding an unlisted increase.
  • Front and back do not align: compare global row number and cable-repeat phase before seaming.
  • Finished garment is too narrow or wide: compare the washed gauge and actual piece width with section 01; if they differ, change needle size rather than stitch counts.
  • Rib band ripples or pulls: redistribute pickup spacing and, if necessary, change the total by a multiple of 4.
